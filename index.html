About

I’m a PhD student in neuromorphic computing at the University at Buffalo, focused on hardware-efficient machine learning — spiking and weightless neural networks, ML–hardware co-design, and in-memory computing. The throughline of my work is making intelligence cheap to run: fewer MACs, lower power, architectures that fit the hardware.

Before the PhD I earned a B.S. in Computer Engineering at Buffalo and spent two years as a software engineer at Axon building large-scale cloud data infrastructure.

Research

Recurrent Differentiable Weightless Networks

Added recurrence to weightless networks — a LUT-only family that replaces MACs with table lookups, where each trained lookup table maps directly onto a physical FPGA LUT-6. 82.2% on Spiking Heidelberg Digits (vs. 67% feedforward) and 97.75% on N-MNIST; ran end-to-end on FPGA at 0.6 W via a PyTorch→Verilog export flow.

PyTorchVerilogFPGA

Hardware-Efficient RF Device Fingerprinting

An SNN that fingerprints radios straight from raw IQ samples using biologically-inspired spatiotemporal receptive fields with LIF/LI dynamics. Matched a CNN baseline on 30-device LoRa classification (99% validation), with metric-learning embeddings hitting 0.98 AUC and 92% cross-manufacturer generalization for open-set rogue detection.

SNNNorsePyTorch

Multi-Objective NAS for SNNs

An LSTM-controller architecture search (REINFORCE) that trades off accuracy, spike sparsity, and temporal-coding quality (CV-ISI, Van Rossum distance). A gated reward avoids no-spike collapse; on Spiking Heidelberg Digits the controller converged decisively, beating random search under matched compute.

RLNASPyTorch

CUDA Neural Network Optimization

Extended Hugging Face training to cut unnecessary host–device transfers — keeping generated samples on-GPU and syncing only on mismatch for a ~20% end-to-end speedup. Overlapped H2D/D2H transfers with compute via pinned memory and multi-stream execution, profiling to close GPU-idle gaps.

CUDATritonPyTorch

SRAM Physically Unclonable Function

Designed a 6T-SRAM array exploiting charge-sharing and process variation for PUF-based cryptographic key generation, with transistor-level schematics and layouts in 45nm. Characterized bit stability and readout margins via Monte Carlo simulation and post-layout parasitic extraction.

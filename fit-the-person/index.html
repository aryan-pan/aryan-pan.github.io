Scaling model fitting in PsyNeuLink

Our PsyNeuLink backend distributes parameter evaluations, participant fits, and training simulations across Dask workers. This browser demo illustrates individual fitting.

An educational DDM illustration, not a benchmark. The page uses 525 precomputed drift-diffusion models: drift v from 0 to 2 and symmetric boundaries ±a from 0.4 to 1.6, with noise scale 1, a 0.01-second time step, and 0.2 seconds of non-decision time. Each model uses 4,000 simulated trials. Sample participants each have 200 separately simulated trials.

For your task data, choices are coded as correct or incorrect relative to the motion direction. The stimulus has 25% coherent motion. Three practice trials are followed by 24 scored trials; responses faster than 0.2 seconds are repeated, and unanswered trials time out after four seconds. Moving a slider selects a precomputed prediction. “Fit” evaluates the joint choice/response-time histogram log likelihood at every grid point; the brief animation shows that search, not measured computation time. The curve shows the marginal response-time histogram. Scores use smoothed histogram probabilities. The match meter is a relative visual guide, not a statistical probability. This page does not execute PsyNeuLink, Dask, or a neural likelihood. The browser fit does not estimate uncertainty. Generalization, hierarchical estimation and benchmark performance are discussed on the poster.

Created for Aryan Pandey's US-RSE poster on distributed and hierarchical parameter estimation in PsyNeuLink. Task responses are kept in browser memory only and are not uploaded. Refreshing clears your responses. With few trials, fixed non-decision time and browser/device timing, the parameters are only approximate task-specific estimates. After an initial online visit, supported browsers cache the demo for offline use.
